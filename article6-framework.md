# Paris Agreement Article 6 Framework

Article 6 of the Paris Agreement is the international rulebook for carbon markets. It allows countries to voluntarily cooperate to achieve their emission reduction targets (Nationally Determined Contributions or NDCs) through the transfer of carbon credits.

## Article 6.2: Bilateral Cooperation

Article 6.2 allows countries to trade emission reductions and removals with one another through bilateral or multilateral agreements.
- **ITMOs**: These traded carbon credits are called Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs).
- **Corresponding Adjustments (CA)**: The most critical mechanism of Article 6.2. When Country A sells an ITMO to Country B, Country A must add those emissions back to its ledger, and Country B deducts them. This prevents double counting on a sovereign level.

## Article 6.4: The UN Carbon Market

Article 6.4 establishes a centralized, UN-regulated global carbon market (the successor to the Kyoto Protocol's Clean Development Mechanism). 
- It allows public and private entities to trade carbon credits generated from specific, verified projects.
- Credits generated under this mechanism are expected to command a premium due to strict UN oversight.

## Impact on the Voluntary Carbon Market

The operationalization of Article 6 is causing a paradigm shift in the VCM.
As sovereign nations enter the market to purchase ITMOs, a severe supply deficit for high-integrity removal credits is anticipated. CRBN Credit's analysis indicates that credits authorized for Article 6 transfer will trade at a significantly higher tier than standard voluntary credits.

CRBN Credit tracks corresponding adjustments and Article 6 authorizations in real-time, allowing users to filter marketplace inventory for ITMO-compliant assets.
