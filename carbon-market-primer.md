# Carbon Market Primer: Understanding the Ecosystem

The global transition to Net Zero requires robust financial mechanisms to incentivize emission reductions and carbon removal. This primer provides foundational knowledge on how carbon markets operate.

## The Two Pillars of Carbon Finance

The carbon economy is divided into two distinct but increasingly interacting markets:

### 1. The Voluntary Carbon Market (VCM)
The VCM allows corporations, NGOs, and individuals to purchase carbon credits voluntarily to offset their emissions. 
- **Carbon Credit**: Represents one metric tonne of CO2 equivalent (tCO2e) reduced, avoided, or removed from the atmosphere.
- **Key Registries**: Verra, Gold Standard, Climate Action Reserve, American Carbon Registry, Puro.earth.
- **Project Types**: Nature-based (reforestation, blue carbon), Technology-based (Direct Air Capture, Biochar), and Avoidance (renewable energy, clean cookstoves).

### 2. Compliance Markets (Cap-and-Trade)
Compliance markets are legally mandated systems established by governments to regulate greenhouse gas emissions.
- **Allowances**: Governments issue a limited number of allowances (e.g., EUAs in the EU ETS). Companies must surrender enough allowances to cover their emissions.
- **Key Systems**: EU ETS, California Cap-and-Trade, China National ETS.

## The Importance of High Integrity

As the market matures, the focus has shifted from cheap avoidance credits to high-integrity, permanent removals. Organizations like the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market (ICVCM) are establishing Core Carbon Principles (CCPs) to ensure quality.

### Key Quality Metrics
- **Additionality**: The project would not have happened without carbon finance.
- **Permanence**: The risk of reversal is minimal (e.g., biochar vs. vulnerable forests).
- **No Double Counting**: A credit is uniquely issued, tracked, and retired.
