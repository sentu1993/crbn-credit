# CBAM Explained: Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is a landmark tool put in place by the European Union to put a fair price on the carbon emitted during the production of carbon-intensive goods that are entering the EU.

## Why CBAM?

As the EU raises its climate ambition and the carbon price in the EU ETS increases, there is a risk of **carbon leakage**—companies moving production to countries with less stringent climate policies. CBAM levels the playing field by ensuring that imported goods pay a carbon price equivalent to what they would have paid if produced under the EU ETS.

## How it Works

1. **Declarations**: EU importers must declare the embedded emissions in their imported goods.
2. **Certificates**: Importers must purchase and surrender CBAM certificates corresponding to the declared emissions.
3. **Pricing**: The price of CBAM certificates is directly tied to the weekly average auction price of EU ETS allowances (EUAs).
4. **Deductions**: If a carbon price was already paid in the country of origin, that cost can be deducted from the CBAM obligation.

### Covered Sectors
Initially, CBAM focuses on sectors with high risk of carbon leakage:
- Cement
- Iron and Steel
- Aluminum
- Fertilizers
- Electricity
- Hydrogen
