# Climate Finance Trends (2026 and Beyond)

Climate finance is the fuel driving the global transition to Net Zero. We are observing several structural shifts in how capital is deployed into carbon reduction and removal projects.

## 1. Shift from Avoidance to Removal

Historically, climate finance poured into "avoidance" projects (e.g., building a wind farm instead of a coal plant). Today, the market recognizes that to achieve true Net Zero, we must physically remove historical CO2 from the atmosphere.
Capital is aggressively reallocating toward engineered removals (Direct Air Capture, Biochar, Enhanced Weathering). Despite current high costs ($200-$1000/tonne), forward-purchase agreements are funding rapid technological scaling.

## 2. Advance Market Commitments (AMCs)

Organizations like Frontier (backed by Stripe, Alphabet, Shopify, McKinsey) are utilizing Advance Market Commitments. By guaranteeing to buy a certain amount of carbon removal at a set price years in the future, they provide project developers the bankability needed to secure construction loans today.

## 3. Integration of Nature and Tech

"Nature-based" and "Tech-based" solutions are no longer viewed in opposition. Hybrid approaches, such as Biochar (processing organic waste into stable carbon) or Biomass Carbon Removal and Storage (BiCRS), are receiving massive private equity inflows due to their balance of high permanence and scalability.

## 4. On-Chain Climate Finance (ReFi)

The use of smart contracts to deploy climate finance directly to local communities (bypassing traditional NGO overhead) is scaling rapidly. Decentralized MRV (Measurement, Reporting, and Verification) using satellite data and IoT sensors is reducing project verification costs by over 40%.
