# Compliance Markets Overview

Compliance carbon markets are regulatory cap-and-trade systems representing over $1 Trillion in traded value annually. Unlike the voluntary market, participation is mandatory for covered entities.

## Cap-and-Trade Mechanics

A government or regulatory body sets a "cap" on total allowable greenhouse gas emissions for specific sectors. 
- The cap is divided into allowances (e.g., 1 allowance = 1 tonne of CO2).
- The cap is reduced annually, forcing overall emissions down.
- Companies that reduce emissions below their allocation can sell excess allowances.
- Companies that exceed their allocation must buy additional allowances.

## Major Global Systems

### The EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS)
The largest and most mature compliance market. It covers power generation, heavy industry, and aviation within the EU. The EU ETS sets the benchmark global carbon price (often trading between €60 - €100).

### California Cap-and-Trade
Administered by CARB (California Air Resources Board), this system covers roughly 80% of the state's GHG emissions and is linked with the cap-and-trade system of Quebec.

### China National ETS
The world's largest system by covered emissions, focusing primarily on the power generation sector.

## Convergence of Compliance and Voluntary Markets

While distinct, the boundary between compliance and voluntary markets is blurring.
- **CORSIA**: The aviation sector's offsetting scheme bridges the gap by allowing airlines to purchase high-quality VCM credits to meet compliance obligations.
- **Article 6**: Governments are beginning to purchase VCM credits as Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs) to meet their Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement.
