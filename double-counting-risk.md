# Double Counting Risk in Carbon Markets

Double counting is one of the most critical threats to the environmental integrity of global carbon markets. It occurs when a single greenhouse gas emission reduction or removal is used more than once to demonstrate compliance or achieve mitigation targets.

## Forms of Double Counting

1. **Double Issuance**: When two different registries issue carbon credits for the exact same underlying project.
2. **Double Use**: When a single carbon credit is used twice (e.g., claimed by two different corporate entities against their Net Zero targets).
3. **Double Claiming**: When a carbon credit is claimed by a corporation in the VCM, but the host country *also* counts that same emission reduction toward its sovereign Paris Agreement target (NDC).

## The Sovereign Threat: Corresponding Adjustments

Under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, double claiming is addressed through **Corresponding Adjustments (CA)**. 
If a carbon credit is exported from Country A to Country B (or to a corporation), Country A must make an adjustment to its national ledger, adding the emissions back to its own total. This ensures the reduction is only counted once globally.

However, tracking Corresponding Adjustments is complex and politically sensitive. Many countries are hesitant to authorize credits for export, severely impacting VCM supply dynamics.
