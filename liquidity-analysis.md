# Liquidity Analysis in the Carbon Economy

Liquidity—the ability to buy or sell an asset quickly without significantly affecting its price—remains a core challenge in the Voluntary Carbon Market (VCM).

## Current Liquidity Constraints

Unlike compliance markets (EU ETS) where millions of EUAs are traded daily in highly liquid centralized exchanges, the VCM behaves more like the real estate or private equity markets.
- **Bespoke Assets**: Carbon credits are non-fungible. They possess unique attributes (vintage, geography, methodology, co-benefits).
- **OTC Dominance**: Over 80% of VCM transactions are executed Over-The-Counter (OTC) through brokers, leading to slow execution times and wide bid-ask spreads.
- **Stranded Assets**: Older vintages (pre-2018) or credits generated under scrutinized methodologies (e.g., some legacy renewable energy projects) have essentially zero liquidity as institutional buyers refuse to hold them.

## The Role of Tokenization in Unlocking Liquidity

The emergence of "Regenerative Finance" (ReFi) and tokenized carbon is fundamentally altering liquidity dynamics. By bridging carbon credits to blockchains (like Polygon or Celo), they can be pooled into standardized liquidity tokens (e.g., BCT, NCT).

This allows the creation of Automated Market Makers (AMMs) like Uniswap, where liquidity providers supply capital to facilitate instant, 24/7 trading of carbon assets. CRBN Credit heavily weighs DEX (Decentralized Exchange) volume in its Fair Value models due to this increasing liquidity depth.

## Institutional Sourcing

For large institutional buyers needing to source hundreds of thousands of tonnes of high-integrity removal credits, traditional OTC liquidity is insufficient. CRBN Credit’s **Institutional Marketplace** aggregates global supply across registries, CEXs, and DEXs to fulfill bulk orders without causing massive slippage.
