# Tokenized Carbon Research

Tokenized carbon refers to digital representations of verified carbon credits on public blockchains. It sits at the intersection of traditional climate finance and Web3, commonly referred to as Regenerative Finance (ReFi).

## The Mechanics of Tokenization

Typically, tokenization works via a "bridge":
1. A traditional carbon credit (e.g., a Verra VCS credit) is retired or immobilized on the legacy registry.
2. A corresponding cryptographic token (an NFT or ERC-20 token) is minted on a blockchain (like Polygon).
3. The token contains metadata proving its origin, vintage, and project details.

### Pooling for Liquidity
Individual carbon tokens are often too bespoke to trade easily. Protocols like Toucan pool similar tokens together (e.g., all nature-based credits from 2016 onward) to create highly liquid, fungible tokens like **NCT** (Nature Carbon Tonne) or **BCT** (Base Carbon Tonne).

## Benefits

- **Democratization**: Retail users and small businesses can buy micro-fractions of a carbon credit.
- **Programmability**: Smart contracts can automatically offset a transaction's carbon footprint in real-time.
- **Transparency**: Every trade, price change, and ultimate retirement is publicly verifiable on block explorers.

## Risks and Regulatory Status

- **Quality Control**: Initially, bridges were used to dump low-quality, "zombie" credits onto the blockchain. Strict quality gates (like Toucan's recent methodology filters) have since been implemented.
- **Regulatory**: Tokenized carbon occupies a gray area. While generally viewed as utility tokens or digital commodities, regulatory bodies are closely monitoring the space.
